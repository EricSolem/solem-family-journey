A family history

From a Norwegian Hillside
to the American Prairie — and Back Again

For centuries, the Solem family lived around Solem, Rognes and Støren, south of Trondheim. In 1880, Ole Sivertsen Solem crossed the Atlantic. In 2024, his descendants returned.

Enter the story

The story in one line

Stay rooted. Then cross an ocean.

Our research reaches back into the 1600s. For generations, the family remained centered in the same small Norwegian landscape. Then one generation changed everything. The story that followed carried the Solem name from Norway to South Dakota, on to California, and eventually back to the farm where it began.

Chapter 1

Roots in Norway — 1600s to 1817

For hundreds of years, the family barely moved from this part of central Norway.

Our current family-tree research reaches back to Peder A. Heggvoll (1634–1721) and Karen Joensdotter (1642–1705), then through Anders P. Heggvoll, Ingeborg H. Vollum, Ole Andersen, Marit E. Solem, Esten Olsen Solem, Siri O. Økdal, Erik Olsen Solem, and onward to Ole Sivertsen Solem.

Chapter 2

Solemsli — the family farm

A place that anchors the story across centuries.

According to the Norwegian farm history, Erik Olsen Solem, born in 1777, courted Gjertru Estensdatter and established a croft on her family's land. Around 1817, the farm buildings associated with Solemsli were established.

The land was worked, animals were kept, and a stream mill operated. Generations grew up in the same landscape.

“The people whose names we had been researching had actually lived here.”

Chapter 3

Life at Solemsli

Work, family and hardship on a Norwegian croft.

Erik and Gjertru's son Ole Eriksen Solem married Marit Henriksdatter. Marit died in 1849; Ole died in 1851. Their children were left without parents, and in February 1852 the poor commission divided the younger children among neighboring farms.

Sivert Olsen, born in 1820 at Litjvollen under Rognes, married Gjertru Olsdatter Solem and took over the croft.

The local history describes Sivert's work at the stream mill, his animals, the garden and the family routines around the millhouse. Around 1870, the mill was still part of family life.

Chapter 4

Ole Sivertsen Solem

Farm boy. King's Guard bugler. Emigrant. Homesteader.

Ole Sivertsen Solem was born March 1, 1853.

At seventeen, in 1870, he served in the Norwegian Army as a bugler with the King's Guard. Family history says he would rather serve in the King's Guard than tend the farm, so he did.

It was exciting work, but it did not pay particularly well. Within a decade, Ole made a decision that would reshape the family history.

1880
Ole leaves Norway for America at age 27.

April 1880

The journey begins in Trondheim

On April 13, 1880, Ole registered to emigrate with his wife Anne Haugen. His parents, Sivert and Gjertru, were also part of the same migration. The recorded destination was Marshall, Minnesota.

Two days later, on April 15, the S/S Tasso departed Trondheim for Hull, England. Current research strongly indicates the Solem family traveled on that sailing.

Solemsli→Trondheim→S/S Tasso→Hull→Liverpool→North America

A birth at sea

Family history records that Ole O. Solem was born at sea on April 18, 1880. The three-day timing makes it possible that he was born aboard the Tasso. That remains a strong inference, not a confirmed fact.

Justin

The family record also says that young Justin died during the journey on the St. Lawrence River. The exact ocean liner and contemporary death record remain unresolved.

1880 research status: The Tasso departure is strongly supported. The family's exact ocean-going vessel from Britain has not yet been identified. We preserve that distinction rather than presenting an inference as fact.

Chapter 5

The American Prairie

From Marshall to Brookings, and from one Norwegian family to a large American one.

Norway → America

The young man who had preferred the King's Guard to the Norwegian farm eventually built a farm of his own in South Dakota.

Family history says the property remained in the family for generations.

Ole and Anne's American children

Family records identify Justin, Ole O., Selmer, Berndt “Ben,” Ella, Gina, Iver and Johanna among the children of the family. Justin died during the migration, Ole O. was born at sea, and Selmer died in his first year.

By 1884, the family was established in Brookings County, South Dakota.

Chapter 6

Jørgine — another journey from Norway

A second Norwegian migration would become central to the American branch.

Jørgine Wahl was born in 1868 and grew up at Ansnes, Norway, the youngest of eight children. Her childhood was marked by loss: her father died when she was four, one brother drowned at sea, and two other brothers died as children.

In 1891, Jørgine emigrated to America and joined her brother in South Dakota. She eventually entered Ole's household as a nanny and housekeeper. In 1900, she and Ole married.

Together they had six children: Justin, Anna, Elmer, Selmer, Olga and Millard.

Chapter 7

Solie and Ruth — South Dakota to California

The next generation carried the family west.

Elmer “Solie” Solem, born in Brookings in 1904, married Ruth Elizabeth Nesvold. Ruth left the Midwest for California. Solie followed. They married in 1936 and established their lives in the San Francisco area.

Their sons were Roger Elliot Solem, born in 1937, and Donald Loren “Don” Solem, born in 1942.

Chapter 8

Roger — basketball, Army and family

A life shaped by athletics, military service and family.

Roger Elliot Solem

Basketball and Cal

Roger became an outstanding basketball player at Capuchino High School in Millbrae, earned national recognition, and was named Male Athlete of the Year in 1955.

He was accepted to the preparatory school for the U.S. Air Force Academy, but a slight astigmatism prevented that path. He chose UC Berkeley instead. At Cal, he remained close to basketball and was part of the practice-squad environment during the era of the 1959 NCAA championship team.

Roger & Margaret

Fort Bliss, marriage and the All-Army team

After Cal, Roger entered the U.S. Army. While stationed at Fort Bliss in El Paso, he met Margaret Rhea at the Officers Club.

They married in 1961 at the William Beaumont Army Hospital chapel. Roger later played basketball for the All-Army team out of Milwaukee.

After leaving the military, Roger and Margaret returned to California. Their children were Laura (1963) and Eric (1966).

A generational echo

Roger nearly followed the Air Force Academy path himself. Thirty years later, his son Eric graduated from the U.S. Air Force Academy in 1989 and became a pilot.

Roger died August 28, 2017, at age 80. He was later buried with military honors in Southern California.

Chapter 9

Don — politics, public affairs and family

A major California career — and a key link back to Norway.

Donald Loren “Don” Solem

California politics and public affairs

Don followed Roger to UC Berkeley and studied political science. At only 26, he became executive director of the California Democratic Party in 1968.

He later served as George Moscone's chief of staff and, in 1976, founded Solem & Associates. Over his career, he participated in more than 300 campaigns and helped build a firm with more than 1,400 clients.

Don and his wife Anne lived in Mill Valley beginning in 1971. Anne served two terms as mayor. Don remained active in education, youth sports and nonprofit organizations.

Family bridge

Don's return to Norway

Don's importance to this family history goes beyond his career. His 2008 return to Norway helped preserve a connection to the extended Norwegian family and to the ancestral farm.

Chapter 10

An earlier return — 2008

Don and Anne returned to Norway, met extended family, and visited the ancestral farm.

Don and Anne traveled through Norway with their daughter Katherine and reconnected with members of the extended Norwegian family. Among those they met were Finn and Tormod Røe, descendants of a related Norwegian branch.

They also spent time with relatives around Rognes. More importantly, Don stood again among the buildings associated with the family his grandfather Ole had left in 1880.

Chapter 11

Finding the way back — 2024

Barbara's connection. Krister's help. Solemsli found.

2008

Don and Anne return to Norway

Their trip, family connections and visit to Solem became part of the trail the next generation could follow.

2024

Barbara connects Eric with Krister

Barbara Barnes, daughter of Anna Solem Bjelde and granddaughter of Ole Sivertsen Solem and Jørgine Wahl, had traveled to Norway herself and connected with Norwegian journalist Krister Olsen. Barbara put Eric in touch with Krister before the 2024 trip.

2024

Oslo → Flåm → Sognefjord → Bergen → Trondheim

Eric, Jennifer, Luke, Sydney and Zach followed much of the same route before turning south toward Støren, Rognes and Solem.

With Krister's knowledge of the area, local history and maps, the search narrowed through Støren and Rognes to Solemsli.

In 1880, Ole left this landscape for America. In 2024, 144 years later, his descendants were standing on it again.

Chapter 12

The book in the farmhouse

The history was waiting where the history happened.

The property is now owned by Jon Solem, who welcomed the family to the farm. Inside the farmhouse was a local history volume by Hermann Aune:

Bygdefolk og Bygdeliv i Støren
Bind II

The book contains photographs, farm histories and stories from the Støren area. It documented people and places directly connected to the Solem line, including Gjertru and Sivert Solem, their croft, the stream mill, and the old Solemsli property.

Source note: The local-history book and inherited genealogy pages are primary evidence of what the family possessed and preserved, but the inherited genealogy page contains handwritten corrections and ambiguities. Where it conflicts with parish records, emigration registers or other stronger sources, those conflicts remain noted rather than silently reconciled.

Preserving the family story

Barbara Barnes

Much of this history survived because earlier generations kept records — and later generations kept asking questions.

Barbara Barnes, daughter of Anna Solem Bjelde and granddaughter of Ole Sivertsen Solem and Jørgine Wahl, was especially helpful in pulling together the family's American history and researching her grandmother Jørgine's life and journey from Norway to South Dakota.

Barbara had traveled to Norway herself and developed connections there. Most importantly for the 2024 journey, Barbara put Eric in touch with Krister Olsen. That introduction proved instrumental in locating Solemsli.

The 2024 discovery was therefore not the work of one generation. It was the continuation of research, relationships and memories passed forward over many years.

The circle closes
